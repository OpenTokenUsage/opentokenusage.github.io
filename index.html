When it speaks up

  • Usage levels

    A notification at the levels you pick, for every provider or each on its own, once per limit until it resets. The bars show a notch at each level.

    80% and 95% by default

  • Pace and resets

    When you're on pace to run out before a reset, and when a limit you'd nearly used up resets. A weekly limit's pace can count work days only.

  • Sounds

    Windows' default, eight built-in sounds or your own file. Quiet during Do Not Disturb.

  • One-click fixes

    A provider you're signed out of shows a quiet card with its sign-in button. Errors come with the fix: run the login command in a terminal, or open the provider's API key page and Windows' Environment Variables. A Claude or Codex card that lost its sign-in refreshes the moment you sign in again.

  • Service status

    A badge on the card when the provider's status page reports an incident (for providers with a public status page).

See where your tokens go

Open a provider for its detail page. For Claude and Codex it adds what only their own logs on your PC can tell, and none of it leaves the PC.

What it costs

  • Usage by project

    Each repository's share of the month and its cost at API prices, with its git branches and any ticket key in the branch name.

    Claude, Codex

  • Monthly budgets

    A project's bar turns yellow at 80% and red at 100%, with a notification at each, even with the panel closed. The list shows where the month is heading.

  • Export CSV

    Saves the month to Downloads, one row per branch.

  • Your currency

    Costs in one of 13 currencies, at a rate you enter. No exchange-rate service is contacted.

  • Spend summary

    Under the cards: today, yesterday and the last 30 days across Claude, Codex and other CLIs' own logs, the models you used most, and, with your plan prices set, how many times its price each plan was worth.

    Claude, Codex, CLIs

When and how you use it

  • Usage by hour

    Tokens per weekday and hour over the last 4 weeks, with your busiest hour.

    Claude, Codex

  • Year of use

    Every day of the last year with your streaks, kept after Claude Code deletes its logs. Export daily tokens as CSV or JSON.

    Claude, Codex

  • Live sessions

    The sessions running on this PC, on their cards: the model and thinking level each runs on, working or waiting for you, the time left on Claude's prompt cache, and how full each context is, with a warning before it compacts.

    Claude, Codex

  • Banked resets

    How many rate limit resets you hold, what each one clears and when it expires, with reminders 7 days and 24 hours before.

    Claude, Codex

  • Cache hit rate

    How much of the last 30 days came from the prompt cache.

    Claude, Codex

Costs come from ccusage, which runs through Bun or Node.js. Without either, the lists show tokens.

Make the taskbar yours

Pick what sits next to the clock in Settings. The taskbar strip is experimental: Windows has no official place for it, so a Windows update may move or hide it.

  • Tray icon

    The app icon, a percent (colored by pace if you like), bars, or one number or logo ring per provider.

  • Taskbar strip

    Logos with session and weekly numbers inside the taskbar, in your fonts, sizes, colors and order, beside the tray or on the other side, on one monitor or all of them.

    Up to 6

  • The panel

    Opens in the corner of the screen from the tray, the strip or a global shortcut. Pin it above other windows, drag an edge to widen it, or drag it anywhere and it opens there next time.

  • Auto refresh

    Smart checks every 2 minutes while your usage moves and slows down to 15 when it doesn't. Or pick a fixed interval.

    5 to 60 min

On your desktop too

Settings → Taskbar & desktop puts the strip's providers on the desktop, in the same look.

  • Desktop widgets

    Side by side, stacked, one per provider or each in turn. Drag them anywhere; they snap to screen edges and to each other, can stay on top, lock in place or let clicks pass through, and hide while a full-screen app is in front.

  • Screen-edge rail

    Today's tokens and spend, each provider in a ring filled as far as its usage, and the live token rate, tucked against the right, left or top edge until the pointer comes near.

Your usage stays on your PC

  • No telemetry

    The app sends no analytics. Usage goes from each provider to your PC and nowhere else. Besides the providers you turn on, it contacts only their status pages, GitHub for updates, and npm for ccusage, which prices Claude and Codex logs.

  • Local HTTP API

    On by default: other apps on your PC can read your usage. Loopback only; browsers are refused.

    127.0.0.1:6736

  • Hide from screen sharing

    The panel and the widgets stay out of screen shares, recordings and screenshots.

  • Proxy

    Route provider, status page and update requests through an HTTP, HTTPS or SOCKS5 proxy, set in Settings → App (details).

  • Updates

    Checks for a new release every 15 minutes and downloads it. It installs quietly, without admin rights, when you click Restart to update, and the panel opens again.

  • Open source

    MIT licensed. Every line is on GitHub.

Questions

  • How do I see my Claude Code usage limits on Windows?

    Install OpenTokenUsage and stay signed in to Claude Code. The app uses Claude Code's existing login, so there's no API key to set up, and shows your 5-hour session limit and weekly limit next to the clock, with reset times, pace and extra usage. Its detail page adds what each project costs and when in the week you use it.

  • How do I check my Codex usage limits?

    Sign in to Codex as usual. OpenTokenUsage reads that login and shows the 5-hour and weekly limits, the code review limit, credits and banked resets, and can warn you before you run out.

  • Is there a CodexBar for Windows?

    CodexBar and OpenUsage live in the macOS menu bar. OpenTokenUsage does the same job in the Windows 11 notification area: it's built on OpenUsage, inspired by CodexBar, and made for Windows.

  • Which AI coding tools does it track?

    Claude, Codex, GitHub Copilot, Cursor, Antigravity, JetBrains AI Assistant, Devin, Kiro, OpenCode Go, Z.ai, Kimi Code, Kimi API, Factory (Droid), MiniMax, Grok, Amp, Synthetic, Muse Code, OpenRouter, DeepSeek, Codebuff, Kilo, Qwen Code, Ollama, and API balances from New API, One API, Sub2API or your own endpoint. Each one is a plugin, and new ones arrive with app updates.

  • Can it warn me before I hit a limit?

    Yes. It notifies you at the usage levels you pick (80% and 95% by default, or your own for each provider), when you're on pace to run out before the reset, and when a limit resets, with a sound of your choice.

  • Does it send my usage or logins anywhere?

    No. There's no account and no telemetry. Your logins stay on your PC, and the app talks only to the providers you turn on, their status pages, and GitHub for updates. For Claude and Codex costs it runs ccusage, which Bun or Node.js downloads from npm.

  • Why does Windows warn me about the installer?

    The installer isn't code-signed, so a setup downloaded in the browser gets a Microsoft Defender SmartScreen warning: choose More info, then Run anyway. winget and the app's own updates don't show it. The app installs for your Windows user only, so it never asks for admin rights.

  • Is it free?

    Yes. It's free and open source under the MIT license. Install it with winget or the installer from GitHub Releases. If it saves you time, you can buy me a coffee.

Install it in one line
